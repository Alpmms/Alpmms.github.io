01 — About

I am a PhD researcher in Civil & Environmental Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, working with Prof. Erol Tutumluer at the intersection of transportation geotechnics and data-centric, interpretable machine learning for civil infrastructure.

My work asks how models trained on large engineering datasets can be made trustworthy: resistant to data leakage, honest about their limits, able to generalize across climate zones and heterogeneous infrastructure conditions, and interpretable in physically meaningful terms. Recent work drew on 82,682 laboratory measurements from 603 independent LTPP pavement sections across 40 U.S. states to develop and evaluate interpretable models for resilient-modulus prediction, using section-based partitioning to prevent leakage and SHAP to surface physically meaningful drivers.

Alongside pavements, I work on seismic site characterization of critical infrastructure, post-disaster ground-damage assessment, and machine learning for climate-sensitive geosystems such as permafrost. Before the PhD I completed dual M.S. degrees in geotechnical engineering (SIUE and Karabük University, with an Erasmus+ exchange at ESTP Paris) and worked as a transportation and geotechnical engineer on roadway and large-scale construction projects.

02 — Research

Reliable learning from real infrastructure data

My projects share one thread: dependable inference from large, messy, real-world engineering datasets — validated honestly and interpreted in ways an engineer can act on. The work spans four connected areas.

R1

Data-centric & interpretable ML for infrastructure

I develop pavement and infrastructure models judged not only on accuracy but on how they were validated and whether their behavior can be explained. My LTPP resilient-modulus framework uses section-based train/test partitioning to remove the data leakage that inflates reported performance, and SHAP analysis to confirm the model's drivers are physically sensible — extended to national-scale pavement deterioration under climate variability.

R2

Seismic & critical-infrastructure resilience

I characterize the seismic exposure of critical facilities and turn it into screening tools: a global Vs30-based site characterization of nuclear power plant sites, a compound seismic screening index combining probabilistic rock-PGA hazard with site conditions, and nonlinear site-response analysis of CPT-based soil profiles comparing near-field and free-field behavior.

R3

Machine learning for climate-sensitive geosystems

Cold-region infrastructure is a moving target as permafrost degrades. I combine remote sensing — MODIS land-surface temperature — with machine learning to model permafrost change and its geotechnical implications, integrating domain knowledge so predictions stay interpretable to engineers.

R4

In-situ characterization & geotechnical modeling

A recurring aim is extracting engineering value from simple field tests. My thesis built regression models linking dynamic cone penetration (DCP) data to strength and dynamic soil parameters; related work covers rockfall and rock-slope stability assessment and finite-element modeling of soil–structure interaction in PLAXIS and ABAQUS.

Selected research highlights

Large-scale, leakage-resistant ML

An interpretable resilient-modulus framework built on 82,682 measurements from 603 independent LTPP sections across 40 states, with section-based partitioning to prevent leakage and SHAP for physically meaningful attribution.

Climate & domain heterogeneity

Evaluation of model behavior across climate zones and heterogeneous infrastructure conditions, emphasizing data composition and realistic out-of-section generalization over headline accuracy.

AI for climate-resilient infrastructure

Machine-learning approaches to permafrost degradation and its infrastructure implications, integrating geotechnical domain knowledge with data-driven modeling.

Data-driven critical-infrastructure assessment

Global seismic site characterization of nuclear power plant sites using USGS Vs30 data, and research on AI-supported post-disaster ground-damage assessment.
